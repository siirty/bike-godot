What this experiment does and does not establish

The opponent schedule is fitted offline against a nominal prescribed-power rider, then frozen as power/braking/slot intentions. Every replay uses the production 50 ms Rust race kernel, the same terrain, initial positions and fixed capabilities. The schedule is never repaired during replay.

Legacy geometry gates (not a learning-validity score): ≥80% of a phase within ±10% FTP of the target, mean mechanical demand within ±20% FTP, intended formation present ≥50% of the phase, and physiology shortfall ≤1% of requested work. These provisional bounds are explicitly diagnostic, not validated training-contract semantics. “Formation” means a usable named wheel in Z1/Z2, a pacer behind you in Z3, or the strong move within 0–25 m during harder efforts. Acquiring the wrong formation is reported as failure even when your prescribed watts were delivered.

The legacy named-wheel follower is phase-informed. The new public-cue reader receives no phases, target watts, reserve or author intent; its rules and motor controller remain provisional. Neither establishes human recognition. Prescribed-power fidelity is a control condition, not proof of induced effort. Mechanical demand is an instantaneous estimate at the target's speed/acceleration, not a proof that every cheaper strategy fails. “Safe decoy” is an authored hypothesis: compare ignore versus cover outcomes. Tactical legibility and race-deciding significance still need human inspection.

All zones means Z1 recovery, Z2 endurance, Z3 tempo, Z4 threshold, Z5 VO₂, Z6 anaerobic and Z7 sprint. CP=FTP and the chosen W′/peak values are explicit synthetic fixtures. No real trainer, measured activity, full evaluator, general scenario generator, collision avoidance, or runtime Scene Planner is included.

Local playback; no network dependencies. Re-run the Rust tool to change FTP, mass or seed. No practice observation becomes an activity or skill score. JSON, frozen opponent inputs and CSV are exported alongside this page.
